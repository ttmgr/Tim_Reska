Doctoral thesis

Nanopore Environmental Metagenomics for One Health: A Tiered Framework for Detection and Attribution

I build sequencing pipelines that find pathogens and antibiotic resistance in the environment. Then I tested whether 28 AI models could build the same pipelines. Not one built the harder pipeline correctly, and many wrote code that looks right and is wrong.

Since July 2026 I work in medical underwriting. I judge health risk from medical evidence, calibrate risk surcharges on large, noisy data, and build disease progression models.

Genomic surveillance for One Health

Nanopore sequencing workflows that detect pathogens, resistance genes and their hosts in air, water, food and clinical samples. I was a founding member of an international research group that built its lab and computing setup from scratch across 7 sites. Seven publications came out of it, including one in Nature Communications.

    Air monitoring by nanopore sequencing

    Reska T, Pozdniakova S, Urban L. ISME Communications

    Nanopore sequencing can profile the microbes in air down to species level, even from the small amounts of fragmented DNA an air sampler collects. First data from Barcelona point to stable air microbiomes specific to each location.

Against Plausibility: can AI build a real genomics pipeline?

My published pipelines gave me something rare: a correct answer to check AI against. I asked 28 models, most of them from the OpenAI, Claude and Gemini families, to build two of them step by step: one that can be solved and one that cannot. I scored all 476 steps on tool choice, parameters, output compatibility, scientific validity and whether the code runs. The models were tested between February and April 2026.

  • No model builds a fully correct 10-step wetland pipeline.In three of its steps, not one of the 28 models gave a fully correct answer.
  • GPT-5, Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Pro are the first in their families to complete the 7-step air pipeline.9 of the 28 models got full marks on every step of this pipeline.
